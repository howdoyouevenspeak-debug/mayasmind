AVOIDANCE


2026.011.10

Hello Blog.
I’m sorry for have being away for so long.

Anyway, today I wanted to talk about something that’s been troubling me. I sort of distilled it into an AP seminar leading question: how do you determine if distancing oneself from something that incites anxiety is based in avoidance or self preservation?

For example, I’m choosing to not eat corn because I don’t like the texture of corn. Any dish with corn in it, I don’t eat.

Or I don’t go into the city because I don’t like loud sounds.

Or I don’t watch horror movies because they scare me.

Or I don’t like driving cars so I stick to riding the bus.

How do you determine if you’re avoiding something vs. doing something out of self preservation?

Maybe one has to look at their own values like family, work, etc. If the act of moving away from doing something interferes with that value, it’s probably avoidance.

For example, you begin missing work because of the intricate bus routes you take everyday to avoid driving. You might get fired.

Or all of your friends are watching a horror movie together and invite you, but you decline since it scares you. You see that they all had a fun time together went out to bowl afterwards. You feel FOMO.

On the other hand, if you do something and it doesn’t interfere with things you care about, then it’s not that helpful. I say it is self-preservation.

For example, no one is hurt or changed if you don’t eat corn. Well, unless grandma makes a 10-day corn casserole that you are pressured to eat because you don’t want to see your cute grandmother sad.

Or your job is in the countryside and you live in the country. All of your family is in the country. You have no reason to go to the city for anything so it doesn’t matter if you choose to not go to the loud, bustling city.

Basically, if it makes your life, your family’s life, or your friends’ lives harder to not do something, then you should learn how to overcome your fears.

If it doesn’t actually matter one way or another, then I think it is okay to choose not to be around it.

What my brother George was saying is that you can tell based on where the fear lies. If you’re scared to drive, then you should get over your fears and drive. If you’re scared to make friends, make a friend. If you are in an abusive household and you’re afraid to leave, then leave. If you are in a family with complex issues and you want to leave to somewhere else because you don’t want to go through any of the emotions, you should stay.

That last point is the reason I’m writing any of this. I have been feeling so many emotions lately that have erupted because of my family. Lots of good ones, lots of bad ones. Sometimes, it seems like too much for me. This week, I had the idea that I’m gonna move to Brooklyn to get away from it all and focus on my own character. I thought no one cares if I’m here or not, but it would matter if I left. I care about my family the most and I want to be included in it. Being in Japan was nice for developing myself and figuring out things, but my family’s life continued without me and that made me sad. I had so much FOMO. So to wrap this point up, I know that if I just went away to a different city for an years, sure I would be focusing more on myself and maybe become more self-reliant, but for me family is the most important thing to me so I know that I’d feel quite lost and ultimately doing harm to myself by avoiding all of the issues that come with family. Plus, those issues that I’d be avoiding would only be filled in with other equally provoking ones.

Though, simultaneously, I do believe that spending time away from your family and exploring the world is important as well. There’s a balance.

Anyway, it was nice to write here again. I have to dry my clothes, read, draw, and go to work. My drawing has stayed stagnant because I haven’t really practiced recently. I’ve been avoiding it because I am anxious I am going to fail and make something that looks bad. Interesting.
